Tell fallen Helvetia, happy once and free, Their bad examples are no guide to thee; Point to thy sister Albion, who hath hurled Her thunders on the troublers of the world, While the vexed continents affrighted bend, And yield.submissive rather than contend; While some portentous change, some plan desigin' By the wise counsel of th' unerring mind, Labours for birth, while truth with pale dismay, Flies from a world where all alike betray. Do thou, with Albion, bid the wretched trust On that firm faith which scorns to be unjust: Say, that, like her, thy sea bound coasts shall formn A port impervious to the wasteful storm; And kindly cherish in thy fertile clime The generous virtues to the end of time. ETNA. SUGGESTED BY READING BRYDONE's TRAVELS. ARISE, Brydone, and from thy glowing page Tear the malignant stain of sceptic rage; Where genius calls, or taste invites thee, rove Over Gela's wastes, through Agrigentum's grove; Pervade the cells where Superstition reigns, And spill what blood the fabling vase contains; Drag from monastic haunts preposterous lies, And shame the bigot drone who living dies; But shun the paths by inspiration trod, Nor doubt the living oracles of God; Nature, obedient to the dread record, Speaks not a language which belies her Lord; And learning, conscious of his power august, Bends to his word or crumbles into dust. for.thee vast themes remain, a boundless store Of deep reflection, or of classic lore; See where Trinacria's nymphs to crown thee choose The garland sacred to their Dorian muse; Rich in unnumbered fruits, the grateful soi Intreats thy presence and applauds thy toil, k2 132 Her ambient streams like veins of silver rul Over her green fields, and sparkle in the sun; While, rich in beauty, Eulia shows from far Those flowers the goddess dropped from Pluto's car. Go, seek where Etna's lofty summit hurled His blazing entrails on the infant world; Over his broad base her mantle Ceres throws, There the fig blackens, and the vintage glows; His middle region, Pan's prolific aid Girds with a belt of variegated shade; There, from the yawning mouths of wasted fires, The pine magnificent to heaven aspires; There, where the torrent of destruction spread, The regal oak now rears his stately head; In vales once blasted by the sulphurous shower, Minerva's olive opens her snowy flower; While through the horrors of the dark defiles, The poplar, proud of spring, luxuriance smiles. Hark, on his lofty summit throned sublime , Mlid ice coeval with the birth of time, Majestic Etna calls thee from afar, To view the shock of elemental war; His sultry fires, for ever though they glow, Break not their bulwark of opposing snow; Nor yet can Winter, who, incensed, appears To scorn the suns of many a thousand years, 133 By humid damps, or frigid blasts, enchain The bold insurgent that invades his reign; On Iis white robe the flaming cinders beam, His walls of frost confine the blazing stream, While glowing rocks by loud explosion rent, Roll their vast ruin down the steep descent; High in their cauldron boil th' infernal waves, While Echo through her subterraneous caves Reverberates along the dark abyss; The loud concussion and electric hiss Shook to the centre of his ample reign The trembling mouutain agonised with pain; From their deep channels lifts his subject floods, O'erturns his vineyards, and unroots his woods; While night, in all her deepest horrors dressed, Throws over meridian Sol her gloomy vest; And, pleased to see the smoky fumes absorb Tile clear resplendence of the radiant orb, She boasts that vast destruction shall again Restore old Chaos to earth's void domain. Such are the views which Etna oft provides For those whom wonder to his summit guides; To thee, Brydone, he gave the awful scene Clad in stupendous dignity serene; When taste adjure thee the proud heights to scale, To see the morning lift her orient veil, 134 When Phcebus, pleased Sicilia to behold, Shakes the wet ocean from his locks of gold, And the glad isle, rejoicing in the sight, Drinks his full beam and kindles into light. Even as the waving flood of glory pours Over mountains , rivers, cities, lakes, and shores, The rapt eye wandering the horizon round, Perceives no limit, no opposing bound; Wondering , it views great nature's self expand, Like a vast picture from its master's hand; Till the mind sinks, enfeebled by amaze, And aching vision quits the painful gaze. AN ADDRESS TO POVERTY. ORN in the northern desert rude, 'Mid the hyena's ruthless brood, Where famished bears incessant prowl, And tonight's silver empress howl, Where winter's unresisted hand Strews tempest over the ice bound land, Oh, Poverty! thy furrowed form Proves thee the daughter of the storm.
