This has been accounted Arbitrary, and not the least Grievance in former Reigns; for indeed an absolute submitting the Press to the will of a Licenser, is bringing the whole Trade of Books, and the whole Body of Learning, under the Arbitrary Power of Mercenary Men. The Labors of the most capable Scholar, the Elaborate Works of the most exquisite Artist, the most Practical Discourse on the Divinest Subject, Dissertations and Transactions in all Sciences shall fall to the Ground, and the Student lose his Years of Labor, and the World the Advantage of his Learning and Parts, unless a sum of Money can be raised to bribe a Mercenary Licenser, and a Hackney Messenger. 'Twould be endless to reckon up the many Volumes on all needful Subjects, which were absolutely rejected in the days of the Press's Restriction, when the most Orthodox Divinity was suppressed, because the Man was not approved that wrote it, and a Book was Damned for the Author, not the Author for the Book. This was a branch of Arbitrary Power in the Government; for in Rightful Power in they do not Tyrannize themselves, but if the Officers are allowed to impose upon them, Under-Spur-leathers are always the Tyrants; a Government regulated by Laws, and Governed according to such Regulations, never willingly put it into the power of any Inferior Officer to Tyrannize over his fellow Subjects. I observe there are sorts of people who are willing to promote a general License, and very studious to defend it; but it is plain they are such as promote Principles in Argument, which they can but very sorrily defend; and flattering themselves, from what Grounds I believe they themselves hardly know; that they may obtain a Licenser to their Advantage, they suppose from thence a liberty to obtrude their preposterous Notions upon the World, and by favor of a Law and an Arbitrary Licenser, partial to their own Factions, suppress the possibility of a reply. These people are in the right to desire such a thing, as a Licenser; for false and designing Reasoning, requires the support of Power to defend it from the invincible force of Truth and Demonstration. But methinks they should be able to see that the present Government is not so suited to those Principles, as that they should expect so Arbitrary and unjustifiable an Office should be erected, after so many Years being laid side.
